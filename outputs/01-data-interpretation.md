# 01 — Data interpretation

Run: interactive, 2026-10-23, fresh run. Every number here is taken from
`outputs/01-data-analysis.md` (generated by `scripts/analyze.py`). All data is synthetic and
must not be presented as real Mirantus figures. Rates are associations, not effects.

## What was done and why

- **Data quality.** n=9000 rows, one per customer, no duplicate IDs, no missing values outside
  the three columns that are empty by design, no impossible dates (no second screening before
  the first or after the export), no `report_opened = false` with a delay, and consistent
  location attributes. No rows were excluded. The only oddity: `report_opened_days_after` takes
  only the values 0, 1, 2, 3, 5 and 9, which looks like a synthetic or bucketed field; it is
  used only in coarse bands. [CSV]
- **Cohort and window.** Follow-up time to the export ranges from 276 to 637 days. The primary
  measure is **return within 457 days (15 months) of the first screening, for customers first
  screened on or before 2025-06-30** (n=3511): every one of them has fully had the 15 months.
  [CSV] I chose 15 rather than 12 months because returns peak around months 11-14: a 365-day
  window counts n=114 returns in its cohort but misses n=173 returns of the same cohort that
  came after day 365 and before the export. A 12-month window measures the edge of the peak,
  not the behaviour. 15 months captures 87.8% of all observed returners by timing. [CSV] The
  trade-off: the cohort shrinks to the first half of 2025. 9-, 12- and 18-month windows are kept
  as robustness checks.
- **Naive rate not used.** The naive rate (any return by export, all customers) is 3.4%
  (n=9000). It is *lower* than the 15-month rate of 4.7% because it mixes in Q3 and Q4
  customers who have not yet reached the return peak; it would also drift upward every month
  without anything changing. The censoring-aware Kaplan-Meier curve gives 4.9% cumulative return
  by month 15, consistent with the window rate. [CSV]

## Findings

### 1. Return is rare, and "on time" cannot be measured from this export
- **Says:** within 15 months, 4.7% of the H1-2025 cohort return (n=3511); by 18 months, 6.5%
  of the Q1 cohort (n=1574). Returns cluster at roughly 10-15 months. [CSV]
- **Does not say:** whether this is a problem. The export has **no recommended-interval
  column**. A customer with a 24-month interval who has not returned is a success so far, but
  is counted as a non-returner in any window rate. Intervals longer than 637 days cannot be
  judged at all from this export; those customers are not judgeable rather than non-returners.
  The 10-15-month cluster is consistent with many customers having a roughly annual interval
  [ASSUMPTION], but the data cannot confirm it. Returns are also only seen at mira locations.
  [CSV]
- **Means for the metric:** "returned at the recommended interval" needs the interval
  captured per customer before it can be a core metric. Until then, the 15-month window rate on
  a cohort that has fully had the window is the most honest baseline. [CSV]
- **Confidence:** high that return within 15 months is low; low on how much of the gap is
  "late" versus "not yet due".

### 2. Customers who do not open the report rarely return, and non-German speakers mostly do not open it
- **Says:** openers 6.0% (n=2281) vs non-openers 2.2% (n=1230). Non-German speakers open at
  37.8% vs 71.8% for German. Once opened, their return rate is close to the German one. Across
  the 2025 cohort, the open-rate gap is about 554 reports that would have been opened at the
  German open rate. [CSV] The report is German only (`data/README.md`).
- **Does not say:** that opening causes return. Engaged customers may both open and return.
  The language-group n for openers is modest (n=228). [CSV]
- **Confidence:** medium-high on the association and on the language open gap; low on
  causality.

### 3. Consent is associated with higher return, and it caps any Mirantus reminder
- **Says:** 5.7% with consent (n=1975) vs 3.5% without (n=1536); this holds within store types
  and within result categories. [CSV] Consent covers 54.2% of all 2025 customers (n=4880).
  Consent or an opened report reaches 84.0% (n=7559); 16.0% (n=1441) have neither. [CSV]
- **Does not say:** that consent drives return. Consenting customers are self-selected and may
  be more engaged. [ASSUMPTION]
- **Confidence:** high on the association and on reach counts; low on effect.

### 4. Phone follow-up is associated with higher return, but rests on few stores and only on consenting customers
- **Says:** phone 8.2% (n=452) vs none 4.0% (n=1939). Email is barely different from none
  (4.6%, n=1120). Phone sits almost entirely in independent opticians (row share 92.7%). Within
  independents, phone customers with consent return at 11.3% (n=256), phone customers without
  consent at 2.5% (n=163). [CSV]
- **Does not say:** that phoning works in general. `optician_follow_up` is self-reported per
  location, not observed per customer. Phone covers only one chain location (n=33, directional)
  and six independent locations, so a few well-run stores can drive the whole cell. The consent
  split suggests opticians may phone only consenting customers [ASSUMPTION], or that consent
  marks engaged customers. Mobile units report no follow-up at all, so follow-up and location
  type cannot be separated there. [CSV]
- **Confidence:** low-medium. It is a lead worth exploring with the qualitative work, not a
  sizing basis.

### 5. Age and result category are associated with return; the biggest absolute pool is unremarkable results
- **Says:** 60+ 6.2% (n=1264) vs 18-39 2.4% (n=788). Minor findings 9.4% (n=742) vs
  unremarkable 3.4% (n=2428). In absolute "gap to best level" terms, the largest pools are
  unremarkable results (about 146 returns), no reported follow-up (about 82), non-openers
  (about 47) and no consent (about 34). [CSV]
- **Does not say:** that unremarkable customers are late. They are the group most likely to
  have longer intervals [ASSUMPTION], so their low window rate may be correct behaviour. Minor
  findings do not return earlier than unremarkable (median 384 vs 372 days), which would be
  odd if they had shorter intervals. [CSV]
- **Confidence:** high on the associations; low on what they mean without intervals.

### 6. Mobile units return least, but they are a small segment
- **Says:** 1.5% (n=199); mobile is 5.1% of all customers (n=460). [CSV]
- **Does not say:** much. Only n=3 returns in the window. It is directional in age splits.
  [CSV]
- **Confidence:** low-medium.

## Questions the data cannot answer

1. What interval did the ophthalmologist recommend for each customer, and so who is actually
   late? (No interval column.)
2. Did any individual customer actually receive optician follow-up, and through which channel?
   (Location-level self-report only.)
3. Does a translated or simpler report raise return, or only open rate? (No variation in report
   language exists.)
4. Do non-returners get eye care elsewhere (ophthalmologist, other screening)? (Only mira
   returns visible.)
5. Did referred customers who booked a partner appointment get a new interval from that
   practice? (Not in the export.)
6. Why do customers with consent and phone follow-up return so much more: the call, the store,
   or the customer? (Requires an experiment or qualitative evidence.)
