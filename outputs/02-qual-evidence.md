# 02 — Qualitative evidence: why screened customers do or do not come back

Step 2 of the Mirantus return-rate case. Written without access to `data/screenings.csv` or any
other output, so the quotes are not bent to fit the numbers. All material is synthetic.

**Inputs read:** `data/interviews/01`–`04` [INT-01]–[INT-04], `data/optician-survey.md`
[SURVEY-1]–[SURVEY-15], `data/customer-journey.md` [JOURNEY], `data/constraints.md`
[CONSTRAINTS] (for the contradictions only), `CASE.md`.

**How to read the quotes.**
- Text in "quotes" is verbatim as it appears in the source file. German is kept verbatim with an
  English translation in [brackets].
- Rows marked *(note)* are the interviewer's notes, not the person's words. They are paraphrase
  by the interviewer and must not be quoted as the person's speech.
- [INT-03] was held in German; quotes marked *[translated by interviewer]* are the interviewer's
  English rendering, not the optician's words. Two English quotes in [INT-03] ("a few", "A
  list. …") are not marked as translated although the interview was in German; treat them as
  interviewer renderings too.
- [INT-04] was held in German and the whole note is "translated by the interviewer": every quote
  from the ophthalmologist is an English rendering, not her verbatim words.
- [INT-02] was held in English; its quotes are verbatim.
- Survey answers are "unedited" per the source; some are in English, some in German. The survey
  does not say which store type gave which answer, except where the answer itself says so.

## 2. Mechanisms

"Independent sources" counts distinct people or documents: each interview is one source, each
survey answer is one respondent, and [JOURNEY] is one source (included in the count where
listed). Survey respondents may share a chain or a region; the survey does not say, so their
independence is not guaranteed. These counts show how many voices raise a mechanism, **not how
common it is**.

### M1. An unremarkable result is read as "I'm healthy, done"
The customer reads the absence of a finding as an all-clear and sees no reason to return.
- Evidence: E02, E03, E04, E45, E49, E58, E60
- Independent sources: 3 — [INT-01], [INT-04], [JOURNEY]

### M2. Nobody ever states a next screening, let alone a personal interval
The report has no next-screening recommendation, the doctor's form has no field for one, and risk
factors that would shorten it are not asked. Where an interval was given, it came from a partner
practice outside mira. Several opticians propose filling the gap themselves with one fixed
"yearly" interval.
- Evidence: E08, E13, E33, E35, E37, E44, E46, E47, E58
- Independent sources: 7 — [INT-01], [INT-02], [INT-04], [SURVEY-6], [SURVEY-8], [SURVEY-10], [JOURNEY]

### M3. No trigger at the moment a return is due
Customers forget; Mirantus sends nothing after the report even to customers who consented;
opticians have the phone channel but no list or memory of who is due, no visibility of who
came back, and often no time.
- Evidence: E05, E14, E17, E21, E22, E25, E26, E30, E36, E60, E61
- Independent sources: 6 — [INT-01], [INT-02], [INT-03], [SURVEY-3], [SURVEY-9], [JOURNEY]

### M4. The messenger decides whether a prompt is heard, and it differs by store type
A long-standing independent customer listens to "her" optician and does not know Mirantus.
A chain customer does not remember the store and wants to hear from whoever holds her results.
Opticians insist on owning contact and want their own logo or their own call.
- Evidence: E01, E05, E06, E11, E15, E19, E20, E27, E28, E31, E38, E42
- Independent sources: 8 — [INT-01], [INT-02], [INT-03], [SURVEY-1], [SURVEY-4], [SURVEY-7], [SURVEY-11], [SURVEY-15]

### M5. The report cannot be understood or acted on
Medical language, German only, behind a login. Customers skim for red, or run it through a
translator, and come away unsure what to do next.
- Evidence: E03, E12, E16, E50, E56, E57
- Independent sources: 4 — [INT-01], [INT-02], [INT-04], [JOURNEY]

### M6. The channel does not reach the customer
Everything after the screening is e-mail. Customers read e-mail "ab und zu", live on WhatsApp,
and opticians say their customers read SMS, not e-mail.
- Evidence: E07, E14, E41, E56, E60
- Independent sources: 4 — [INT-01], [INT-02], [SURVEY-14], [JOURNEY]

### M7. Paying for each repeat, at an interval assumed to be yearly
Price is a hesitation for at least one older customer and, per opticians, for pensioners. It is
not for a younger customer. Opticians earn on glasses, not on the screening, so a repeat that
sells nothing is of little interest to some of them.
- Evidence: E09, E16, E19, E29, E32, E37, E40, E55
- Independent sources: 8 — [INT-01], [INT-02], [INT-03], [SURVEY-2], [SURVEY-5], [SURVEY-10], [SURVEY-13], [JOURNEY]

### M8. The location cannot screen when the customer would come back
Trained examiners leave and screening stops; at chains, the store cannot even decide on
follow-up.
- Evidence: E23, E27, E39
- Independent sources: 3 — [INT-03], [SURVEY-7], [SURVEY-12]

Evidence with no mechanism of its own: E18 (context), E43 (doctor workload, relevant to any form
change), E48 (fear warning: a design constraint rather than a reason for non-return), E51–E53
(where consent and contact exist today), E54 (the in-person moment is unused), E59 (referral
customers leave the loop).

## 3. Contradictions

Stated, not resolved.

1. **Who the reminder should come from.**
   - Customer side: "But not from the optician. I don't even remember which store it was. I'd want it from whoever has my results." [INT-02] vs. "Wenn Herr K. sagt, ich soll wieder kommen, dann komm ich wieder." [If Mr K. says I should come again, then I'll come again.] [INT-01].
   - Optician side: "The customers are mine. If someone writes to them, it's me." [INT-03]; "Bitte keine Mails von Mirantus an meine Kunden." [Please no e-mails from Mirantus to my customers.] [SURVEY-4]; "Der Befund sollte von uns kommen." [The report should come from us.] [SURVEY-11] vs. "Mehr Werbung von Mirantus, damit die Leute wissen, was das ist." [More advertising from Mirantus, so people know what it is.] [SURVEY-15].
   - With constraints: [INT-02] wants it from the results holder; [CONSTRAINTS] allows Mirantus to send it only on the optician's behalf with the optician's name. [INT-03] and [SURVEY-4] reject Mirantus mail at all, while [CONSTRAINTS] lets Mirantus send service messages anyway and [SURVEY-1] accepts an automatic reminder "mit unserem Logo" [with our logo]. Whether an optician-named, Mirantus-sent reminder is acceptable to [INT-03] and [SURVEY-4] is not answered.

2. **Do customers want to be reminded?**
   [INT-03]: "Older customers find it pushy if you call them for something they didn't ask for." vs. [INT-01] (67): "Wenn Herr K. sagt, ich soll wieder kommen, dann komm ich wieder." and [INT-02]: "Honestly I'd love a reminder."

3. **Will opticians do the calling?**
   [SURVEY-3]: "Wir rufen dann selbst an." [We'll call them ourselves.] and [INT-03]: "Then my people can call." vs. [SURVEY-9]: "Wir haben keine Zeit, Kunden anzurufen. Es muss automatisch gehen." [We don't have time to call customers. It has to be automatic.] Within [INT-03] itself: staff could "theoretically" call, "aber wer soll sich das merken?" [but who is supposed to remember that?]. [CONSTRAINTS]: opticians can't be required to do extra work.

4. **Is there a problem at all?**
   [SURVEY-2]: "Nothing, our customers come back for glasses anyway." vs. [INT-03] knows only "a few" came back; [INT-01]: "Warum? War doch alles in Ordnung." [Why? Everything was fine.]; [INT-04]: "It's the 'unremarkable' 60-year-olds who disappear." (A glasses visit is not a screening return; the two may be conflated in [SURVEY-2].)

5. **One interval for everyone vs. a personal interval.**
   [SURVEY-8]: "dass man jährlich kommen sollte" [that you should come yearly]; [SURVEY-10]: "Jahres-Check" [annual check]; [SURVEY-6] example "Nächstes Screening in 12 Monaten" [next screening in 12 months]; [SURVEY-5] and [INT-01] both assume yearly. vs. [INT-04]: intervals "depend on the person", from yearly to every 2–3 years. vs. [CONSTRAINTS]: an interval for a specific customer is a medical statement that must come from the ophthalmologist's evaluation, not marketing copy, which a till poster telling everyone "jährlich" [SURVEY-8] may conflict with.

6. **Referral customers: "in the system" or lost?**
   [INT-04]: "The referral customers I worry about less — they're in the medical system now." vs. [INT-02], a referral customer: unsure how urgent the referral was, told by the practice to come back in a year, "I will forget otherwise", and has received nothing. [JOURNEY] §4: the customer books the practice themselves; mira is not told the interval.

7. **Consent given, no reminder sent.**
   [INT-02]: ticked the reminders box, "Has received nothing since the report" (note). [JOURNEY] §1 offers "I'd like reminders and offers" at booking, while [JOURNEY] §4 says unremarkable/minor results get "no further contact" from Mirantus. The checkbox promises something the journey does not do.

8. **Language captured, not used.**
   [JOURNEY] §1 asks for preferred language at booking; [JOURNEY] §3 says the report is in German. [INT-02] needed the report "in English and in normal words".

9. **Is price a barrier?**
   [INT-01]: "für einmal okay, aber jedes Jahr? Weiß ich nicht." [okay for once, but every year? I don't know.]; [SURVEY-5]: "69 € every year is a lot for pensioners." vs. [INT-02]: would pay again. Different customers; the material cannot say which is typical.

10. **What the optician gets from a return.**
    [INT-03]: offers mira because people come in and "manche kaufen danach eine Brille" [some buy glasses afterwards] and it differentiates the store; [SURVEY-13]: "verdienen wir am Screening wenig" [we earn little on the screening], only interesting with a glasses sale. Both locate the value in glasses; they differ on whether a repeat screening is worth effort when no glasses follow.

11. **Store tools vs. capacity constraint.**
    [INT-03] has a cash-register CRM that might do reminders; [SURVEY-11] wants the report to come from the store. [CONSTRAINTS]: opticians' CRMs and cash registers can't be integrated this quarter. [SURVEY-7]: at chains, HQ, not the store, decides on customer communication.

12. **Fear vs. urgency.**
    [INT-04]: "If you scare people, the ones who come are the anxious ones, not the ones at risk." and [CONSTRAINTS] (no fear-based messaging) vs. [INT-02] "Wasn't sure how urgent the referral was" (note) and [INT-01] scanning only for "was Rotes" [something red]: customers want a clear signal of seriousness. A tension any copy must hold rather than a direct contradiction.

## 4. Gaps — what nobody in this material speaks to

- **Mobile units.** No interview and no identifiable survey answer. Who owns the customer of a screening bus, and who could send a reminder, is unknown.
- **Chain stores from the inside.** Only [SURVEY-7] speaks for a chain, and only to say HQ decides. Nothing from chain HQ.
- **Customers who did come back.** Both customer voices are (so far) non-returners. We don't know what made returners return.
- **Customers with and without consent.** Only [INT-02] mentions consent. Whether [INT-01] gave consent, and whether counter bookings ask for it at all [JOURNEY] §1, is unknown.
- **Non-German speakers beyond one English speaker.** No Turkish, Russian, Arabic or Polish speaker is heard.
- **Minor-findings customers.** No one with a "minor findings" result is interviewed.
- **The customer's view of a reminder sent by Mirantus under the optician's name.** Nobody was asked about the combined form [CONSTRAINTS] allows.
- **Who sets the interval for referral customers,** and whether a mira interval would clash with a practice's instruction ([INT-02]).
- **What e-mail 3 looks like to the customer** (sender name, whether the optician is named). [JOURNEY] does not say, yet [SURVEY-11] and [INT-01] suggest the customer doesn't link it to the store.
- **Whether opticians would use a due-list in practice**, as opposed to saying they would ([INT-03], [SURVEY-3]).
- **Other ophthalmologists.** One evaluating doctor; we don't know if others would fill an interval field or give different intervals.
- **Willingness to answer risk-factor questions at booking or screening** (family history, diabetes, myopia) — needed for a personal interval ([INT-04]), not asked of any customer.

## 5. What qualitative data cannot tell us

- **How big any mechanism is.** Four interviews and fifteen survey answers show that a mechanism exists and how it works. They do not show how many customers it affects, which segment has the largest gap, or how much any fix would move the return rate. Source counts in §2 are counts of voices, not prevalence.
- **Whether opticians' beliefs about customers are true.** "Our customers don't read e-mails" [SURVEY-14], "older customers find it pushy" [INT-03] and "customers come back for glasses anyway" [SURVEY-2] are claims about customers made by opticians. They need checking against behaviour.
- **Whether stated intentions predict behaviour.** "dann komm ich wieder" [then I'll come again] [INT-01] and "I'd love a reminder" [INT-02] are intentions, not observed returns.
- **Whether any optician follow-up causes returns.** The material describes what opticians do or would do; it cannot separate the effect of follow-up from store type or customer type.
- **Representativeness.** The survey is self-selected and the store type of most answers is unknown; interviewees were not sampled to represent anyone.
