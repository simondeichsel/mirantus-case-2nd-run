# Interview 4 — Ophthalmologist, evaluating partner

Synthetic. Notes from a 30-min call, in German, translated by the interviewer.

- Evaluates around 200 mira screenings a month, remotely, in the evenings.
- On intervals (general guidance, "depends on the person"): under 40 without risk factors every
  2–3 years; 40 and over every 1–2 years; family history of glaucoma, diabetes or high myopia —
  yearly.
- "An unremarkable result is a snapshot, not a guarantee. Patients read it as 'I'm healthy'."
- The evaluation form she fills in has no field for a recommended interval. "I would fill it in
  if it were there. It takes five seconds."
- Risk factors like family history aren't asked at the screening, so she often can't tell who
  should come back sooner.
- Warns against fear: "If you scare people, the ones who come are the anxious ones, not the ones
  at risk."
- On referrals: [translated] "The referral customers I worry about less — they're in the medical
  system now. It's the 'unremarkable' 60-year-olds who disappear."
- Reports: "They are written for colleagues, honestly. Not for patients."
